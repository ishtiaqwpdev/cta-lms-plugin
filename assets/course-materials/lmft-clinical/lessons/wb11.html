How to Use This Workbook

Read the theory sections as decision tools rather than lists of definitions. For each vignette, identify the treatment target and maintaining process, select the theory that best explains the process, choose a matching intervention, and define how response will be evaluated. Then apply the GROUP and TEAM sequences to group, referral, consultation, and interdisciplinary questions. Complete the worksheet and all 10 questions before reviewing the rationales.

Core Exam Principle
Theory earns points only when it improves formulation, intervention fit, sequencing, and evaluation. A named technique without a matching clinical target is not theory-informed practice.

Learning Objectives

  • Use clinical theory to organize assessment findings, explain a maintaining process, select a treatment target, choose a coherent intervention, and evaluate whether the formulation is supported by response data.
  • Differentiate the exam-level focus and intervention direction of structural, strategic, Bowen, experiential, attachment-focused, psychodynamic, cognitive-behavioral, humanistic, narrative, and solution-focused approaches.
  • Assess group purpose, composition, readiness, accessibility, confidentiality limits, developmental stage, cohesion, safety, and member fit before and during group treatment.
  • Distinguish consultation, adjunctive service, specialty referral, higher level of care, transfer, and termination and protect continuity when another professional or service is needed.
  • Collaborate with medical, school, community, and mental-health professionals using informed participation, role clarity, minimum relevant information, assigned responsibility, independent judgment, and outcome review.

Why This Topic Matters

Theory questions often tempt candidates to match one vocabulary word to one theory. Strong exam reasoning goes further: it identifies what keeps the problem going, what change process the theory predicts, and what intervention would test that formulation. Group, referral, and collaboration questions add another layer because the candidate must also choose the correct treatment system and preserve safety, continuity, and professional responsibility.

6. Coherent Integration Rather Than Technique Shopping

Integrative treatment may combine approaches, but the combination should remain clinically coherent. The therapist identifies a primary formulation, explains why each intervention targets a relevant mechanism, sequences methods according to safety and readiness, and evaluates whether the expected change occurs. Adding more techniques is not the same as improving treatment.

  • Name the primary target and maintaining process.
  • Select the primary theory or integrated formulation.
  • Choose the smallest intervention that tests the formulation.
  • Check development, culture, language, access, preference, and competence.
  • Measure response and alliance.
  • Revise the formulation, method, treatment unit, or level of care when evidence does not support the plan.

7. Group Purpose, Screening, and Composition

A group is selected because its purpose and change process fit the client—not because several clients share a diagnosis or because group care is convenient. Screening includes goals, readiness, stability, risk, communication capacity, confidentiality expectations, prior group experience, access, culture, developmental fit, and whether the client can participate without undue risk to self or others.

Group DecisionQuestions
PurposeIs the group psychotherapy, support, psychoeducation, skills, task, or another format? What change process is expected?
CompositionWhat level of similarity or diversity supports the purpose? Are age, stage, risk, language, and functioning compatible?
StructureOpen or closed? Time-limited or ongoing? Size, frequency, leadership, emergency plan, and confidentiality explanation?
Readiness and accessCan the client attend, understand, tolerate, communicate, and use the format? What accommodations are needed?
EvaluationHow will attendance, participation, safety, cohesion, skill use, and outcomes be reviewed?

9. Consultation, Referral, Adjunctive Care, and Transfer

ActionUse WhenContinuity Task
ConsultationThe therapist needs expertise or perspective but retains the case and responsibility.Clarify the question, protect confidentiality, document guidance, and integrate judgment.
Adjunctive serviceAnother provider supplies a complementary medical, psychiatric, educational, vocational, or specialty service.Define roles, obtain authorization, coordinate relevant information, and avoid contradictory plans.
Specialty referralA specific service or competence is needed beyond the current therapist’s role or skill.Explain rationale, identify accessible options, make a warm handoff, and follow through.

10. Interdisciplinary Collaboration

Collaboration may include psychiatrists, physicians, schools, occupational or speech therapists, dietitians, substance-use specialists, child-welfare professionals, attorneys, community agencies, and other mental-health clinicians. The therapist remains responsible for clinical judgment and does not assume that team consensus overrides safety, law, ethics, competence, or the client’s rights.

BeforeDuringAfter
Clarify client goals, consent or authority, purpose, roles, information limits, and the therapist’s question.Use clear clinical language, distinguish fact from inference, invite client participation, address disagreement, and assign responsibility.Document disclosures, decisions, tasks, deadlines, unresolved issues, and the next review point.

Worked Example 1: Theory Selection

A parent becomes increasingly critical when an adolescent withdraws; the withdrawal increases the parent’s fear and criticism. A theory-informed answer should identify the circular maintaining process before choosing a method. Attachment-oriented work may access fear and need beneath criticism and withdrawal; structural work may be stronger if hierarchy and subsystem boundaries are the primary problem. The vignette determines which formulation is best supported.

Best Reasoning
Choose the theory that explains the dominant maintaining process—not the theory with the most familiar technique.

Worked Example 2: Referral and Team Coordination

A client develops possible medication side effects and asks the LMFT whether to stop the medication. The therapist assesses urgency and safety, stays within scope, encourages prompt contact with the prescriber or medical service, coordinates with authorization, and continues relevant psychotherapy. The therapist neither prescribes nor abandons the clinical role.

Best Reasoning
Use adjunctive expertise while preserving scope, consent, continuity, and follow-up.

Chapter Summary

  • Use THEORY: identify the target, state the hypothesis, examine the evidence, match the orientation, assess readiness, and review the yield of the intervention.
  • Choose a theory because its maintaining mechanism fits the vignette—not because a single vocabulary word appears in the stem.
  • Use GROUP: clarify the goal, assess readiness, organize the format, explain confidentiality limits, and monitor process and safety.
  • Consultation supports the therapist while responsibility remains; referral or transfer changes who provides a needed service and requires continuity planning.
  • Use TEAM: define the target, verify authority, assign roles, share the minimum relevant information, monitor follow-through, and preserve independent clinical judgment.

Answer Key and Detailed Rationales

How to Review
For each missed item, identify whether the error involved theory-to-technique mismatch, unsafe group selection, premature referral, role confusion, or failure to evaluate the intervention. Write one sentence explaining why the correct response is better supported and better timed than the strongest distractor.

Question 1 (LMFT-WB11-Q001)

Correct Answer: D

A. Theory guides reasoning but does not provide a universal script.

B. Systemic theories generally avoid reducing a relational problem to one person’s blame.

C. Theory-informed treatment still requires ongoing assessment and revision.

D. Correct. A useful theory links observations to a formulation, intervention targets, and evaluation of response.

Exam Strategy: Use theory to explain the maintaining process and select a matching intervention; do not choose a theory only because its terminology appears in the vignette.

Question 2 (LMFT-WB11-Q002)

Correct Answer: A

A. Correct. Structural work focuses on the organization of the family system, including hierarchy, boundaries, and subsystems.

B. Cognitive distortions are more central to cognitive-behavioral approaches.

C. Psychosexual development is associated with classical psychoanalytic theory.

D. Exceptions are especially associated with solution-focused approaches.

Exam Strategy: Look for hierarchy, boundaries, subsystems, coalitions, and enactments. The best response changes organization rather than merely discussing it.

Question 3 (LMFT-WB11-Q003)

Correct Answer: C

A. Reward schedules are associated with behavioral methods.

B. Paradoxical directives are more closely associated with strategic approaches.

C. Correct. Bowen theory emphasizes differentiation, triangles, emotional process, and patterns across generations.

D. Group composition is a group-treatment issue rather than a defining Bowen concept.

Exam Strategy: Look for differentiation, triangles, emotional reactivity, and multigenerational patterns. Choose observation and de-triangling over therapist-led restructuring.

Question 4 (LMFT-WB11-Q004)

Correct Answer: B

A. Historical meaning may be useful, but it does not directly target the described maintaining sequence.

B. Correct. Strategic reasoning focuses on the problem sequence and how attempted solutions may perpetuate it.

C. A genogram may support assessment but is not the most direct intervention for the specified cycle.

D. A generic referral does not address the identified family sequence.

Exam Strategy: Identify the attempted solution or sequence that maintains the problem, then choose a focused intervention that interrupts that sequence.

Question 5 (LMFT-WB11-Q005)

Correct Answer: D

A. Unconscious interpretation is not the primary solution-focused method.

B. Hierarchy change is structural and should still be connected to assessment and goals.

C. A full historical assessment may be useful in some cases but is not required before using clear exceptions and goals.

D. Correct. Solution-focused practice uses exceptions, preferred outcomes, and small observable changes.

Exam Strategy: Use exceptions, preferred future, scaling, and small observable change when the client can identify goals and resources.

Question 6 (LMFT-WB11-Q006)

Correct Answer: A

A. Correct. Screening should address purpose, readiness, stability, safety, participation capacity, and access.

B. Group treatment is not automatically less intensive or appropriate.

C. A diagnosis or symptom alone does not determine group fit.

D. The therapist is responsible for screening and protecting confidentiality; current members should not make admission decisions.

Exam Strategy: Match the client to the group purpose, format, stage, stability, readiness, and access needs before assuming group treatment is appropriate.

Question 7 (LMFT-WB11-Q007)

Correct Answer: C

A. Ignoring harmful behavior can increase shame and undermine group safety.

B. Ending the group is disproportionate before attempting appropriate intervention and repair.

C. Correct. The facilitator protects safety, names the process, reinforces norms, and supports repair without humiliation.

D. This shifts responsibility to the harmed member and may intensify exposure.

Exam Strategy: Protect safety and participation, name the process, reinforce norms, and invite member-to-member work without humiliating or abandoning a member.

Question 8 (LMFT-WB11-Q008)

Correct Answer: B

A. The medical warning signs and competence limits require more than informal monitoring.

B. Correct. Prompt medical and specialty evaluation is indicated, while coordinated family support preserves continuity.

C. Abrupt termination may create abandonment and continuity risks.

D. Providing specialized nutritional treatment is outside the therapist’s competence and scope.

Exam Strategy: Referral is strongest when competence, medical risk, level of care, or a specialized service requires it and continuity is preserved.

Question 9 (LMFT-WB11-Q009)

Correct Answer: C

A. Team participation and general coordination authorization do not automatically justify release of the entire record.

B. Appropriate collaboration may be clinically useful and permitted.

C. Correct. The therapist should define purpose, authorization, roles, and the limited information needed.

D. Using a partner does not resolve authorization, accuracy, or confidentiality concerns.

Exam Strategy: Clarify consent, purpose, roles, minimum necessary information, responsibility, and follow-up; team involvement is not blanket permission.

Question 10 (LMFT-WB11-Q010)

Correct Answer: D

A. Technique switching without reassessment can repeat the original mismatch.

B. A group referral and closure are premature without assessing fit, safety, and continuity.

C. Blaming the family ignores possible formulation, alliance, access, and intervention-fit problems.

D. Correct. The response integrates safety, health, systemic formulation, treatment response, theory selection, alliance, and collaboration.

Exam Strategy: Choose a coherent formulation and intervention sequence, then evaluate response. Integration is not random technique collection.
