How to Use This Workbook

This workbook develops the crisis-reasoning skills tested in the California LMFT Clinical Examination. Read the content in order, practice the SAFER sequence, complete the decision worksheets, and answer all 10 questions before reviewing the rationales. The central distinction is whether the vignette requires more assessment or whether the facts already establish the need for immediate protective, medical, reporting, or emergency action.

Clinical and Legal Scope NoticeThe material is provided for examination preparation and professional education. It is not legal advice, clinical supervision, diagnosis, treatment, or a substitute for consultation with qualified legal, clinical, regulatory, or professional experts.California legal references in this workbook were checked against official sources on July 24, 2026. Setting-specific procedures, exceptions, and local protocols still require current verification.

Learning Objectives

  • Apply the SAFER sequence to distinguish when a crisis vignette requires additional assessment and when the facts already require immediate action.
  • Conduct direct suicide, self-harm, danger-to-others, abuse, grave-disability, medical, and substance-risk assessment and formulate acuity using historical, dynamic, protective, and acute factors.
  • Apply exam-level California rules for child, elder, and dependent-adult reporting, duty to protect, privilege exceptions, and Section 5150 scope while recognizing setting-specific limits.
  • Select the least restrictive safe intervention, realistic safety plan, appropriate level of care, and follow-up while protecting individual safety in couple and family treatment.
  • Document the facts, questions, formulation, consultation, protective actions, handoff, rationale, and continuity plan and eliminate premature-treatment distractors.

Why This Topic Matters

Crisis items test disciplined sequencing under pressure. The therapist must recognize warning signs, ask direct questions, identify legal and scope issues, and respond proportionately. In couple and family cases, systemic understanding remains important, but neutrality never overrides protection of a child, elder, dependent adult, threatened victim, or person at acute risk.

Core Exam Principle
Assess when the facts are incomplete. Act when danger, medical instability, or a legal duty is already established. Stabilize first; process later.

1. The SAFER Crisis Sequence

StepQuestionTypical Action
S - StabilizeIs anyone in immediate danger or medically unstable?Maintain contact, reduce immediate danger, obtain location, activate emergency response, or secure medical care.
A - Assess directlyWhat are the thoughts, intent, plan, means, preparations, behavior, target, timing, and protective factors?Ask direct, specific, nonleading questions; obtain collateral when appropriate.
F - Formulate acuityHow do historical, dynamic, protective, and current facts combine?State the risk formulation and what makes the situation more or less urgent.
E - Execute the responseWhat is the least restrictive safe and lawful intervention?Safety plan, means-safety steps, report, protective contact, crisis team, emergency evaluation, or higher level of care.
R - Record and reconnectWhat must be documented and followed up?Record facts and rationale; confirm handoff, supports, access, transportation, and follow-up.

Factor TypeExamplesExam Use

Do Not Use Labels as Evidence
Terms such as low, moderate, or high summarize judgment. They do not replace the facts, rationale, and plan.

3. Suicide and Self-Harm Assessment

  • Ask directly about passive death wishes, active ideation, intent, plan, access to means, preparations, and recent behavior.
  • Clarify timing, frequency, duration, controllability, triggers, ambivalence, reasons for living, and what has prevented action.
  • Assess prior attempts, interrupted or aborted attempts, nonsuicidal self-injury, intoxication, psychosis, agitation, sleep loss, pain, and recent loss.
  • Distinguish nonsuicidal self-injury from suicidal behavior while recognizing that intent can change and both require assessment.
  • Use collaborative safety planning and means-safety steps when clinically appropriate; a promise alone is not a substitute for assessment and follow-up.
  • Arrange emergency evaluation when the client cannot remain safe, has imminent intent or behavior, is medically unstable, or cannot participate in an adequate plan.
Incomplete ResponseBetter Exam Response
“Promise me you will not do anything.”Assess current intent, plan, means, preparations, behavior, location, supports, and ability to remain safe.
“Think about how much your family needs you.”Use empathic engagement without guilt, then ask direct risk questions.
“Let us process what triggered this.”Address immediate safety and disposition before deeper exploration.

4. Danger to Others and Duty to Protect

Assess the target, threat content, seriousness, intent, plan, means, access, preparations, history, triggers, psychosis, intoxication, impulsivity, protective factors, and immediacy. A vague angry fantasy requires clarification. A serious threat of physical violence against a reasonably identifiable victim can activate California duty-to-protect analysis.

QuestionExam-Level Rule
Is the victim reasonably identifiable?Clarify the person or group, access, location, and immediacy.
Has the client communicated a serious threat of physical violence?When the statutory facts are present, do not delay reasonable protective action for insight work.
What satisfies the protective response?California Civil Code section 43.92 describes reasonable efforts to communicate the threat to the victim or victims and to a law-enforcement agency.
What else may be needed?Emergency assessment, consultation, documentation, and continuity of care may be required in addition to the legal protective contact.

5. Psychiatric Emergency, Grave Disability, and Section 5150

Severe psychosis, mania, delirium, disorganization, wandering into danger, catatonia, or inability to obtain food, clothing, or shelter may require urgent psychiatric or medical evaluation. California Welfare and Institutions Code section 5150 authorizes specified peace officers, designated facility professionals or attending staff, designated mobile-crisis-team members, and county-designated professionals to take or cause a person to be taken into custody when statutory criteria and probable cause are present. An outpatient LMFT who lacks that designation should not claim unilateral authority to impose the detention; the therapist should maintain safety and activate authorized responders.

Scope Rule
The therapist’s lack of detention authority does not eliminate the duty to obtain emergency help. Separate “I cannot personally initiate the hold” from “I must do nothing.”

6. Substance and Medical Emergencies

PresentationPriority

7. Child Abuse and Neglect Reporting

California Penal Code section 11166 requires a mandated reporter to report known or reasonably suspected child abuse or neglect. Reasonable suspicion does not require certainty or a complete investigation. The current statute states that the initial telephone report is made immediately or as soon as practicably possible, with a written follow-up within 36 hours. Reporting duties are individual; notifying a supervisor is not a substitute for the mandated report.

DoDo Not
Use developmentally appropriate, nonleading questions sufficient for safety and reporting.Interrogate the child for proof or a complete chronology.
Explain the limit of confidentiality in language the child can understand.Promise secrecy or promise what authorities will do.
Assess immediate safety, medical needs, and access to the alleged perpetrator.Confront the alleged perpetrator before reporting.
Make the report and document the basis, action, consultation, and follow-up.Transfer the individual duty to a supervisor or colleague.

8. Elder and Dependent-Adult Abuse

California Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630 includes physical abuse, abandonment, abduction, isolation, financial abuse, and neglect within mandated reporting. The responsible agency and timeline vary by setting. For many nonfacility situations, the statute requires an immediate or as-soon-as-practicable report and a written or internet follow-up within two working days. Exam answers should prioritize immediate safety, basic needs, the required report, and protective resources rather than waiting for proof or arranging confrontation.

Important Limitation
Section 15630 contains setting-specific procedures and limited exceptions. This workbook does not replace the statute, BBS guidance, agency protocol, or legal consultation.

9. Intimate Partner Violence, Stalking, and Sexual Violence

  • Privately assess immediate danger, escalation, strangulation history, threats, weapons, stalking, technology-facilitated monitoring, safe communication, children, pets, transportation, and safe housing.
  • Do not confront the alleged perpetrator or require equal responsibility when coercive control or violence is present.
  • Reconsider conjoint treatment when disclosure may be suppressed or retaliation may increase.
  • Support client choice while offering safety, medical, advocacy, shelter, legal, and crisis resources within applicable law.
  • Avoid detailed trauma processing during acute instability.

12. Crisis Documentation

  • Precipitating event, direct statements, objective observations, and relevant collateral information.
  • Questions asked and responses about ideation, intent, plan, means, behavior, target, victimization, or danger.
  • Historical, dynamic, protective, and acute factors and how they informed the formulation.
  • Consultation, reports, protective contacts, emergency services, records, and legal analysis.
  • Rationale for the level of care and why less or more restrictive alternatives were not selected.
  • Client participation, support-person involvement, means-safety actions, transportation, handoff, and follow-up responsibilities.

Memory Aid: ASSESS OR ACT?

ASSESS
Use direct questions when the warning sign is present but intent, plan, means, behavior, target, timing, medical stability, or reporting facts remain unclear.
ACT
Use emergency, medical, protective, or mandated-reporting procedures when preparations, acute behavior, medical instability, specific serious threats, grave-disability facts, or reasonable suspicion are already established.

Worked Example 1: Telehealth Suicide Statement

A client says, “Everyone would be better off without me,” then becomes silent. The therapist knows the client is at home but has not clarified the exact location, intent, plan, means, or whether anyone else is present.

Best First Step
Remain engaged, confirm the client’s current location, and ask directly about suicidal ideation, intent, plan, means, recent behavior, and immediate supports. The warning sign is clear, but disposition cannot be selected until acuity is assessed.

Worked Example 2: Violence in Conjoint Treatment

During a couple session, one partner discloses recent strangulation and retaliation threats. Communication practice and mutual-responsibility framing are unsafe because the presentation suggests coercive violence.

Best First Step
Create privacy for a direct safety assessment, determine safe communication and immediate danger, develop a protective response, and reconsider whether conjoint therapy is clinically appropriate.

Chapter Summary

  • Use SAFER: stabilize, assess directly, formulate acuity, execute the least restrictive safe response, and record and reconnect.
  • Assess when intent, plan, means, target, behavior, timing, medical stability, or reporting facts remain unclear; act when acute danger, medical instability, or a legal duty is already established.
  • Reasonable suspicion supports a child-abuse report; California elder and dependent-adult reporting procedures vary by setting and require current verification.
  • A non-designated outpatient LMFT may need to activate authorized emergency responders even when the therapist cannot personally initiate a Section 5150 detention.
  • Safety, realistic access, means-safety steps, handoff, documentation, and follow-up take priority over insight work, conjoint neutrality, or trauma processing during acute instability.
