Resource 1 — Person-Specific Suicide-Risk Formulation Worksheet

A. Current Suicidal Thoughts and Behavior

  • Passive or active ideation; frequency, duration, intensity
  • Intent, plan, preparatory behaviors, rehearsal, or recent attempts
  • Access to means; timeline and imminence

B. Acute and Chronic Drivers

  • Recent losses, conflicts, legal, financial, or health stressors
  • Psychiatric, substance, trauma, sleep, and pain contributors
  • Historical patterns and prior suicidal behavior

C. Lethal-Means Access and Environmental Factors

  • Firearms, medications, substances, ligatures, or other means
  • Household, workplace, or community access and storage
  • Environmental isolation, intoxication, or unsafe settings

D. Warning Signs and Change From Baseline

  • Observable changes in mood, behavior, sleep, or functioning
  • Statements, preparations, or giving away possessions
  • Deviation from the client’s typical presentation

E. Protective Factors, Supports, and Engagement

  • Reasons for living, values, cultural or spiritual resources
  • Supports, treatment engagement, and help-seeking capacity
  • Collaboration with safety planning and follow-through

F. Uncertainty and Limitations

  • Missing, conflicting, or unreliable information
  • Limits of structured tools or collateral data
  • What would change the formulation if new data emerged

G. Person-Specific Formulation Narrative

Synthesize A–F into a concise, person-specific statement of current risk and context.

H. Action and Reassessment Plan

  • Immediate safety steps, level of care, and consultation
  • Means safety, safety-plan elements, and follow-up timing
  • Triggers for immediate reassessment

Resource 2 — Collaborative Safety-Planning Implementation Guide

Implementation Principles

  • Collaborative, transparent, and culturally responsive
  • Written in the client’s own words when possible
  • Feasible, rehearsed, and linked to real supports and resources
  • Includes lethal-means safety as a distinct step
  • Revisited when risk, context, or engagement changes

Step 2 — Internal coping strategies

Step 3 — People and social settings for distraction

Step 6 — Lethal-means safety

Feasibility, Rehearsal, and Follow-Up Checklist

  • Client understands each step in their own words
  • Steps are realistic given current functioning and environment
  • Contact information verified and accessible to the client
  • Barriers identified (transportation, language, privacy, disability, cost)
  • Backup options documented if the first step fails
  • Lethal-means plan specifies method, responsible person, timing, and verification
  • Plan rehearsed or walked through in session
  • Supports informed only with appropriate consent
  • Follow-up date and reassessment trigger documented
  • Plan updated in the clinical record and shared per policy

Resource 3 — Level-of-Care and Emergency-Escalation Decision Support

Local Pathway Worksheet

Escalation Triggers Requiring Immediate Reassessment

  1. New or escalating suicidal intent, plan, preparatory behavior, or attempt
  2. Inability to maintain safety or engage a feasible safety plan
  3. Access to lethal means combined with current ideation or intent
  4. Severe agitation, psychosis, intoxication, or medical instability affecting judgment
  5. Loss of protective supports, sudden change, or imminent access to means
  6. Clinician judgment that current outpatient care cannot contain foreseeable risk

Resource 4 — Suicide-Risk Documentation Template and Worked Example

Documentation Template (use in practice)

Fictional Abbreviated Worked Example

Client: Adult outpatient; telehealth session. Reports increased passive death wishes over two weeks after job loss. Denies current intent or plan but endorses access to medication stockpile. Protective: engaged spouse, prior therapy alliance, reasons for living related to children. Structured inquiry completed; no preparatory behavior reported. Consultation with supervisor; collaborative safety plan initiated including means restriction with spouse, 988 and mobile crisis numbers, and next-day follow-up. Documented rationale for outpatient management with enhanced monitoring; reassessment if intent escalates or plan emerges.

Resource 5 — Postvention and Clinician-Support Protocol Checklist

A. Immediate Facts and Safety

  • Confirm known facts; avoid speculation
  • Assess ongoing safety for other clients/staff if applicable
  • Secure immediate environmental needs

B. Required Notifications and Consultation

  • Notify supervisor/program leadership per policy
  • Legal/risk consultation as indicated
  • Coordinate required notifications without violating confidentiality

C. Documentation and Record Integrity

  • Preserve records relevant to care provided
  • Document timeline, contacts, and decisions
  • Avoid altering records inappropriately

D. Continuity of Care and Communication

  • Identify affected clients/families per policy
  • Plan trauma-informed communication
  • Coordinate community/postvention resources

E. Clinician Support

  • Access peer consultation and supervision
  • Monitor personal reactions and countertransference
  • Use EAP or professional support when indicated

F. Operational and Clinical Review

  • Participate in agency review as required
  • Identify systems learning without premature blame
  • Update policies/training needs if assigned

G. Follow-Up and Professional Recovery

  • Schedule follow-up support check-ins
  • Monitor secondary traumatic stress and burnout
  • Resume caseload adjustments as clinically appropriate
