Chapter 1

Telehealth Foundations

BIG IDEA | California Business and Professions Code section 2290.5 defines telehealth broadly as a mode of health-care delivery and includes synchronous and asynchronous methods. California law preserves scope limits and applies professional-responsibility, unprofessional-conduct, and standards-of-practice rules to telehealth.

What to Learn

  • Explain and apply California Definition of Telehealth.
  • Explain and apply Scope and Professional Standards.
  • Explain and apply Therapist Traveling Out of State.
  • Explain and apply Client Location and Interstate Authority.
  • Explain and apply Beginning-of-Session Requirements.
  • Explain and apply Initiation Requirements.
  • Explain and apply Balancing Benefits and Limitations.

Core Study Rules

ConceptControlling Study RuleMemory Cue
California Definition of TelehealthCalifornia Business and Professions Code section 2290.5 defines telehealth broadly as a mode of health-care delivery and includes synchronous and asynchronous methods.Do not reduce telehealth to video therapy. Look for the broader statutory definition.
Scope and Professional StandardsCalifornia law preserves scope limits and applies professional-responsibility, unprofessional-conduct, and standards-of-practice rules to telehealth.When an answer suggests that technology changes scope or lowers standards, eliminate it.

Client Location and Interstate AuthorityUnder 16 CCR section 1815.5(e), a California licensee or registrant may serve a client in another jurisdiction only when the therapist meets that jurisdiction's requirements and telehealth is permitted there.When the client crosses state lines, pause and verify legal authority before relying on continuity or convenience.
Beginning-of-Session RequirementsSection 1815.5 requires the therapist to verbally obtain and document the client's full name and present address at the beginning of each telehealth session and to assess appropriateness.The phrase 'present address' is a strong exam clue. Do not substitute the address already in the record.
Initiation RequirementsThese are the initiation requirements stated in 16 CCR section 1815.5(c) and Business and Professions Code section 2290.5.Look for the complete initiation set: consent, risks/limitations, credential information, and local resources.
Balancing Benefits and LimitationsSound telehealth judgment balances access and continuity against the effect of disruptions on assessment, treatment, safety, and client welfare.California-style questions often reject both extremes. Choose the response that assesses and balances competing factors.
Ongoing AppropriatenessSection 1815.5 requires an appropriateness assessment each time telehealth is provided, and the current psychosocial and environmental changes are directly relevant.A change in symptoms or environment signals an each-session reassessment question.

Technology Failure and Continued AppropriatenessThe therapist should use clinical judgment, consider privacy and effectiveness, and implement the planned alternative when appropriate.The best answer is usually assessment plus a reasonable backup response, not automatic continuation or automatic termination.
California Registrant Serving Out-of-State ClientThe client's Nevada location triggers Nevada's practice requirements, while the APCC must also remain compliant with California registration, employment, and supervision rules.For associates, analyze both jurisdictions and preserve all supervision requirements.
Temporary Practice AllowanceThe allowance is limited, applies to qualifying independent licensees and existing clients, lasts 30 consecutive days, and may be granted only once per calendar year.The words 'existing client visiting California' suggest the narrow allowance, but never assume it is automatic.
Unknown Location During Acute RiskThe therapist needs the present location to coordinate immediate safety action and should address location and risk without unnecessary delay.In acute-risk telehealth questions, location is not paperwork; it is part of the safety intervention.
Technology CompetenceProfessional competence includes understanding the tools used in practice, their limitations, and their effect on confidentiality, safety, and treatment.Licensure and experience are not substitutes for specific competence.
Permanent Interstate Relocation and ContinuityClient need and continuity are important, but they do not override Arizona's authority to regulate services delivered to a person located there.The exam may make the unauthorized option feel compassionate. Choose lawful continuity planning rather than unauthorized continuation.

Telehealth Informed Consent

BIG IDEA | The statute permits verbal or written consent and requires documentation of the consent. Section 2290.5 requires the client to be informed and consent obtained before telehealth delivery.

What to Learn

  • Explain and apply Form of Telehealth Consent.
  • Explain and apply Timing of Consent.
  • Explain and apply Transition to Telehealth.
  • Explain and apply BBS Credential Disclosure.
  • Explain and apply Documentation of Consent.
  • Explain and apply Complete BBS Initiation Requirements.
  • Explain and apply Separate Form Not Required.

Core Study Rules

ConceptControlling Study RuleMemory Cue
Form of Telehealth ConsentThe statute permits verbal or written consent and requires documentation of the consent.Eliminate answers that impose a written-form requirement not found in the statute.
Timing of ConsentSection 2290.5 requires the client to be informed and consent obtained before telehealth delivery.The statutory phrase is “before the delivery” of telehealth.
Transition to TelehealthThe change from in-person treatment to video initiates telehealth and triggers the consent and BBS initiation duties.A change in delivery mode can trigger telehealth initiation even when treatment itself is not new.
BBS Credential DisclosureSection 1815.5(c)(3) requires the credential type and number.When the question asks what must be provided, look for the exact BBS credential information.
Documentation of ConsentThe defect is not the verbal form; it is the absence of documentation.Separate the form of consent from the documentation requirement.
Complete BBS Initiation RequirementsObtain and document verbal or written telehealth consent, explain potential risks and limitations, provide credential type and number, and document reasonable efforts to identify relevant resources including emergency services in the client's geographic area. This answer contains the four initiation requirements in section 1815.5(c).Memorize the four-part initiation set rather than selecting a partially correct checklist.
Separate Form Not RequiredThe law does not mandate a separate form if the comprehensive process satisfies the telehealth requirements.Do not confuse a required process with a required document format.
Generic Signature Is InsufficientA generic signature cannot substitute for informing the client, obtaining telehealth consent, documenting it, and providing the BBS-required information.A signature is evidence only of what the document and surrounding process actually covered.
In-Person Care After Telehealth ConsentIn-person care remains possible, although the particular option depends on availability, legality, and clinical appropriateness.Do not turn the right to receive in-person care into a guarantee that one provider must offer every format.
Local Emergency ResourcesDocumentation of reasonable efforts to ascertain contact information for relevant resources, including emergency services, in the client's geographic area. This is the express local-resource requirement in section 1815.5(c)(4).Choose the precise local-resource requirement rather than a broader crisis-practice recommendation.
Material Change and Updated DiscussionA material platform change should prompt a tailored update to the informed-consent process and documentation.The trigger is material change, not merely the passage of time.
Client MisunderstandingThe client's misunderstanding and changed willingness require renewed discussion, clinical assessment, and documentation.When the client reveals a misunderstanding, repair the informed-consent process before choosing an extreme action.
Consent vs. Each-Session DutiesCalifornia separates initiation consent from the recurring full-name and present-address requirement.Identify whether the question tests initiation duties or each-session duties; they are related but not interchangeable.
Recording ConsentPenal Code section 632 generally requires all-party consent for recording confidential communications.Whenever recording appears, stop treating the question as ordinary telehealth consent.
Voluntary Participation and Limited OptionsThe therapist should support informed choice while honestly addressing the limits of the therapist's practice and continuity needs.Do not confuse client autonomy with a requirement that one therapist personally provide every preferred option.
Effect of NoncomplianceSection 2290.5(d) expressly classifies failure to comply as unprofessional conduct.Choose the consequence the statute actually states, not an exaggerated remedy.
Integrated Telehealth Informed ConsentComplete and document the missing telehealth initiation process, explain material platform and recording issues, address the client's questions and voluntary choice, evaluate lawful alternatives or referral, and document the corrective actions. This answer addresses initiation consent, BBS disclosures, material changes, recording consent, client autonomy, alternatives, and documentation.Integrated questions require a response that fixes every material defect without relying on a signature or an extreme action.

  • Eliminate answers that impose a written-form requirement not found in the statute.
  • The statutory phrase is “before the delivery” of telehealth.
  • A change in delivery mode can trigger telehealth initiation even when treatment itself is not new.
  • When the question asks what must be provided, look for the exact BBS credential information.
  • Separate the form of consent from the documentation requirement.
  • Memorize the four-part initiation set rather than selecting a partially correct checklist.
  • Do not confuse a required process with a required document format.
  • A signature is evidence only of what the document and surrounding process actually covered.

Telehealth Privacy and Security

BIG IDEA | Privacy concerns access and observation, confidentiality concerns professional use and disclosure, and security concerns administrative, physical, and technical protection. Section 1815.5(d)(3) expressly requires compliance with applicable privacy, confidentiality, and security laws and identifies the CMIA and HIPAA security standards.

What to Learn

  • Explain and apply Privacy, Confidentiality, and Security.
  • Explain and apply BBS Telehealth Security Requirement.
  • Explain and apply Secure Platform Limitation.
  • Explain and apply CMIA and Federal HIPAA.
  • Explain and apply Client Environment.
  • Explain and apply Therapist Environment.
  • Explain and apply Unexpected Third Party.

Core Study Rules

ConceptControlling Study RuleMemory Cue
Privacy, Confidentiality, and SecurityPrivacy concerns access and observation, confidentiality concerns professional use and disclosure, and security concerns administrative, physical, and technical protection.Define the problem before solving it. The exam may use one word while the facts involve all three.
BBS Telehealth Security RequirementSection 1815.5(d)(3) expressly requires compliance with applicable privacy, confidentiality, and security laws and identifies the CMIA and HIPAA security standards.Look for the regulation’s actual duty, not an invented product list, guarantee, or repeated authorization.
Secure Platform LimitationTelehealth privacy requires attention to the physical environment as well as the technology.When the facts involve an open room or visible screen, do not let the word “encrypted” distract you.
CMIA and Federal HIPAAThe CMIA is California law, while federal HIPAA directly applies to covered entities and business associates; California BBS telehealth rules also expressly reference both frameworks. This statement accurately distinguishes the frameworks while recognizing the BBS telehealth regulation.Avoid the common distractor that treats HIPAA as the only source of confidentiality law.
Client EnvironmentThe therapist should address the active exposure and use clinical judgment to restore privacy or modify the session.The best first response to environmental exposure is usually pause, assess, and restore privacy.
Therapist EnvironmentThe therapist should control foreseeable environmental technology risks before continuing.Environmental risk includes devices that listen, not only people who are physically present.
Unexpected Third PartyThe therapist should determine who is present and whether the client can voluntarily and privately continue.Unexpected presence calls for clarification before either continuing or taking an extreme action.
Vendor Marketing ClaimsMarketing language must be verified through risk analysis, contractual review, configuration, and other compliance steps.“Certified” and “compliant” are marketing clues. Look for independent assessment rather than blind reliance.
Workplace Device and NetworkThe therapist should educate, explore safeguards, and assess appropriateness.A private room can still contain an insecure or monitored device. Evaluate both layers.
Business Associate AgreementA BAA is generally required for a covered entity’s business associate, but it is only one part of compliance.Do not choose an absolute. Analyze covered-entity status, vendor function, contract, and safeguards.
Recording and TranscriptionThe therapist must separately analyze consent, purpose, security, retention, and vendor handling.Treat recording and transcription as separate data-creation events, not ordinary telehealth features.
Lost Device Incident ResponseImmediate containment and fact assessment are necessary to determine risk and required next steps.Incident first steps are containment and assessment, not waiting, deleting, or making unsupported conclusions.
Unauthorized Participant and Exposure AssessmentThe therapist should contain, investigate, document, consult, and notify according to the facts and governing requirements.For sophisticated incident questions, choose the complete response that separates containment from legal breach determination.
HIPAA Scope and California BBS DutiesThe answer distinguishes direct federal HIPAA scope from California’s own confidentiality and telehealth regulatory requirements.This is a dual-framework question. Do not collapse federal HIPAA status and California BBS duties into one issue.
Coercion and Hidden ListenerThe therapist should assess privacy and safety carefully without escalating a potentially controlling situation.When coercion may be present, privacy assessment is also a safety assessment. Avoid actions that could escalate danger.
Encrypted Cloud Storage and BAAHHS guidance states that a cloud provider maintaining encrypted ePHI can be a business associate even without the decryption key.The key verb is “maintains.” A no-view cloud vendor can still be a business associate.
Integrated Privacy and Security ResponseThe response addresses the active incident, environmental privacy, recording, vendor compliance, documentation, and legally required follow-up.Integrated questions reward the answer that addresses every material problem in a lawful sequence without relying on one document or an extreme reaction.

  • When the facts involve an open room or visible screen, do not let the word “encrypted” distract you.
  • Avoid the common distractor that treats HIPAA as the only source of confidentiality law.

Technology, Electronic Communication, and Professional Responsibilities

BIG IDEA | California telehealth includes asynchronous delivery of health-care services, and symptom assessment plus treatment guidance is clinical care. The governing principle is reasonable safeguards and protection appropriate to the communication and risk.

What to Learn

  • Explain and apply Electronic Communication Categories.
  • Explain and apply Email and Reasonable Safeguards.
  • Explain and apply Communication Expectations.
  • Explain and apply Electronic Health-Service Records.
  • Explain and apply Client-Initiated Email.
  • Explain and apply Group Messaging Disclosure.
  • Explain and apply Unread Crisis Message.

Core Study Rules

ConceptControlling Study RuleMemory Cue
Electronic Communication CategoriesCalifornia telehealth includes asynchronous delivery of health-care services, and symptom assessment plus treatment guidance is clinical care.Look at the purpose and content, not merely the fact that technology was used.
Email and Reasonable SafeguardsThe governing principle is reasonable safeguards and protection appropriate to the communication and risk.Avoid absolute words such as “never,” “only,” and “automatic” when official guidance uses a reasonable-safeguards standard.
Communication ExpectationsClear expectations reduce unsafe assumptions about availability, monitoring, and emergency use.Communication policies clarify expectations; they do not erase professional duties.
Electronic Health-Service RecordsCalifornia permits electronic records, and significant communications should be preserved or incorporated as part of responsible documentation.Technology changes the form of the record, not the importance of clinically significant information.
Client-Initiated EmailHHS guidance permits a limited inference of acceptability while preserving the provider’s responsibility to address risks and safeguards.Client choice informs the analysis but does not transfer professional responsibility.
Group Messaging DisclosureVisible recipients may reveal identifying information and a treatment relationship, creating a confidentiality concern.“No diagnosis was included” does not eliminate the risk of revealing who receives services.
Unread Crisis MessageThe policy helps define monitoring expectations, but once the therapist becomes aware of a suicidal communication, the therapist should assess and respond appropriately.Distinguish not seeing a message from seeing it and choosing not to act.
Clinical DocumentationThe message materially affected assessment and treatment and should be documented accordingly.Ask whether the message changed assessment, treatment, risk, or follow-up.
Client Preference for Unencrypted EmailDiscuss the material risks and alternatives, determine whether the method can be used with reasonable safeguards for the proposed content, honor reasonable communication preferences when lawful, and document significant decisions. This response respects access needs while preserving legal, security, and professional judgment.The strongest answer balances access, client preference, risk, and safeguards rather than choosing an absolute.
Response BoundariesThe therapist should clarify expectations and explore the clinical meaning and treatment impact of the messaging pattern.For nonemergency boundary concerns, the exam usually favors clarification and clinical discussion before an extreme action.
Recipient VerificationVerifying authority, recipient, attachment, and method is a reasonable safeguard.The exam favors verification before transmission rather than repair after a preventable error.
Asynchronous Clinical CareCalifornia’s telehealth definition includes asynchronous delivery of health-care services, and individualized clinical guidance may fall within that definition.When the therapist assesses or treats through technology, think beyond video and consider asynchronous telehealth.
Preserving Risk CommunicationThe therapist must address the imminent clinical concern and create an appropriate record of the message, assessment, actions, consultation, and follow-up.A request for secrecy does not remove risk-management and documentation responsibilities.
Personal Device UseThe appropriate response is a fact-specific assessment and reasonable safeguards, which may require changing settings, access, workflow, or device use.When the facts show manageable risks, choose assessment and correction rather than an unsupported automatic conclusion.
Couples Messaging and ConfidentialityThe scenario requires careful application of the treatment unit, consent, confidentiality policy, safety, and clinical judgment rather than an automatic promise or disclosure.Complex electronic communication does not replace the underlying informed-consent and confidentiality analysis.
Actually Received Emergency MessageThe therapist has actual knowledge of a potentially lethal overdose and should respond promptly; the automated warning does not end the duty to act on known risk.A disclaimer is preventive guidance, not a substitute for action after actual notice of danger.
Integrated Electronic Communication ResponsibilitiesReevaluate the communication system; establish clear purposes, boundaries, response times, and emergency procedures; use appropriate safeguards and approved channels; incorporate clinically significant content into the record; and seek consultation when needed. This response addresses communication design, informed expectations, confidentiality, boundaries, emergencies, documentation, and professional judgment.In an integrated question, select the answer that repairs the entire system rather than relying on preference, prohibition, or a disclaimer.

  • Look at the purpose and content, not merely the fact that technology was used.
  • Avoid absolute words such as “never,” “only,” and “automatic” when official guidance uses a reasonable-safeguards standard.
  • Communication policies clarify expectations; they do not erase professional duties.
  • Technology changes the form of the record, not the importance of clinically significant information.
  • Client choice informs the analysis but does not transfer professional responsibility.
  • “No diagnosis was included” does not eliminate the risk of revealing who receives services.
  • Distinguish not seeing a message from seeing it and choosing not to act.
  • Ask whether the message changed assessment, treatment, risk, or follow-up.

Technology Risk Management and Compliance

BIG IDEA | Risk analysis determines what could threaten electronic information and how serious the risk may be; risk management reduces and monitors the identified risk. Availability means authorized users can access needed information and systems when required.

What to Learn

  • Explain and apply Risk Analysis vs. Risk Management.
  • Explain and apply Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
  • Explain and apply Incident vs. Breach.
  • Explain and apply Safeguard Categories.
  • Explain and apply California Telehealth Security Standard.
  • Explain and apply Access Control and Accountability.
  • Explain and apply Business Associate Agreements.

Core Study Rules

ConceptControlling Study RuleMemory Cue
Risk Analysis vs. Risk ManagementRisk analysis determines what could threaten electronic information and how serious the risk may be; risk management reduces and monitors the identified risk.When the question asks for the distinction, look for “identify and evaluate” versus “reduce and monitor.”
Confidentiality, Integrity, and AvailabilityAvailability means authorized users can access needed information and systems when required.Security is not only secrecy. Lost or inaccessible records point to availability.
Incident vs. BreachAn incident triggers prompt containment and investigation; notification depends on the applicable legal definition and the facts.Do not confuse “respond immediately” with “automatically report to every authority.”
Safeguard CategoriesRisk analysis, policy, governance, and workforce training are administrative safeguards.Administrative safeguards organize people and processes; physical safeguards protect spaces and devices; technical safeguards control systems and data.
California Telehealth Security StandardSection 1815.5(d)(3) expressly requires compliance with applicable law and references the CMIA and HIPAA Security standards.A waiver, product label, or practice size does not replace the BBS telehealth standard.
Access Control and AccountabilityUnique user identification and role-based access help establish who accessed information and what each person is authorized to do.Shared credentials usually signal authentication, auditability, and least-privilege problems.
Business Associate AgreementsA vendor that maintains ePHI on behalf of a covered entity is generally a business associate; the BAA is required but does not replace risk analysis and due diligence.“Stores PHI on behalf of the practice” is a strong business-associate clue.
Lost Encrypted DeviceContainment and factual assessment come first; encryption status is a major factor in determining whether information was unsecured or compromised.Encryption is important evidence, not permission to ignore a lost device.
Wrong Recipient IncidentPrompt containment, evidence preservation, scope assessment, mitigation, consultation, and notification analysis are appropriate.Wrong-recipient questions favor containment and assessment, not waiting, concealment, or additional disclosure.
Addressable Security SpecificationsAddressable requires a documented, context-specific determination and an appropriate safeguard or equivalent measure.On the exam, translate “addressable” as “evaluate, implement or use an equivalent, and document” - not “optional.”
Backup and RecoveryRecovery testing is necessary to determine whether the contingency plan actually supports availability and continuity.A backup is only useful when it can be restored.
Vulnerability and Change ManagementA vulnerability should be assessed and corrected even before confirmed unauthorized access; documentation and monitoring support accountability.Distinguish a vulnerability from a confirmed breach: correct the risk promptly without inventing facts.
HIPAA Breach Risk AssessmentThese are the four factors required by the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule’s compromise analysis.Memorize the four factors: information, recipient, acquisition/viewing, mitigation.
BBS Incorporation of Security StandardsThe BBS regulation expressly incorporates the CMIA and HIPAA Security standards into the telehealth practice requirements.This question separates federal HIPAA status from the independent California BBS telehealth requirement.
AI Vendor Risk ManagementThe tool performs multiple high-risk functions and requires a complete legal, vendor, clinical, and security analysis before use.When a tool records, stores, analyzes, and trains on data, analyze every function rather than the product label.

Exam Reasoning Checklist

  • When the question asks for the distinction, look for “identify and evaluate” versus “reduce and monitor.”
  • Security is not only secrecy. Lost or inaccessible records point to availability.
  • Do not confuse “respond immediately” with “automatically report to every authority.”
  • Administrative safeguards organize people and processes; physical safeguards protect spaces and devices; technical safeguards control systems and data.
  • A waiver, product label, or practice size does not replace the BBS telehealth standard.
  • Shared credentials usually signal authentication, auditability, and least-privilege problems.
  • “Stores PHI on behalf of the practice” is a strong business-associate clue.
  • Encryption is important evidence, not permission to ignore a lost device.
